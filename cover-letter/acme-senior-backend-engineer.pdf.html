Dear Acme Engineering Team,

Acme’s move from a legacy hardware ecosystem to a cloud-native platform caught my attention. The Core Systems role brings order processing, real-time inventory and automated trap-triggering into one reliability problem, with an especially clear constraint: spring-loaded boxing gloves must fire in under 10 milliseconds. That is a far more memorable brief than a generic API modernization, and I like that the work connects software behavior to physical outcomes.

At Jellyfish (formerly Tradelab), I spent six years building backend applications for high-volume programmatic bidding. I joined as a Senior Backend Engineer and became Senior Backend Team Lead, leading three backend engineers and owning technical decisions and delivery. Earlier, as CTO at One Heart Communication, I owned architecture and delivery of a custom CRM and coordinated a hybrid team of six developers. Those roles taught me to combine hands-on design with pragmatic decisions about reliability, delivery, and how teams operate.

My backend toolkit includes TypeScript and NestJS, PHP/Symfony, PostgreSQL, Redis, RabbitMQ, AWS, GCP, Kubernetes, Docker and Terraform. While I have not worked on ballistics or rocket-skate telemetry, I have designed backend applications for high-volume workloads and worked across the database, messaging and infrastructure choices that shape distributed systems. I’m drawn to the role’s high-throughput microservices and low-latency REST and gRPC APIs, as well as its practical resilience work around circuit breakers, rate limiters and fail-safes. I would bring a careful approach to failure modes rather than assume a happy path.

I also built The First Spine, a solo-developed board game project, using NestJS, Docker and Ansible. It is a small example of my preference for owning a system end to end, from architecture to a working product. The chance to apply that ownership to Acme’s unusual mix of software and physical systems, while helping replace a legacy platform, is what makes this role particularly interesting to me.
